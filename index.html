Microkinetic Modeling of Hydrogen Evolution Reaction (HER) via CATMAP

National Institute of Technology Srinagar — Department of Chemical Engineering

Executive Summary: Applied microkinetic modeling using the open-source CATMAP framework to screen electrocatalysts for the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction (HER) in acidic media. Constructed activity volcano plots to validate the Sabatier principle, identifying Platinum (Pt) as the benchmark while evaluating non-precious alternatives like Molybdenum Disulfide (MoS₂) and Tungsten Carbide (WC) for low-cost green hydrogen production.

Key Screening Results

Catalyst Material ΔGH* (eV) H₂ Rate (mol/s) Coverage (θH) Primary Kinetic Bottleneck
Platinum (Pt) -0.02 8.19 × 10-4 0.550 Near-optimal adsorption/desorption balance
Palladium (Pd) -0.05 6.07 × 10-4 0.622 Slight desorption limitation
Nickel (Ni) -0.15 2.23 × 10-4 0.818 Sluggish Tafel/Heyrovsky product desorption
Gold (Au) +0.35 3.02 × 10-5 0.029 Inefficient Volmer proton adsorption

Engineering Insights

  • Sabatier Validation: Peak catalytic activity is achieved when hydrogen adsorption free energy (ΔGH*) is near 0 eV, maintaining intermediate surface coverage (θH ≈ 0.2–0.8 ML).
  • Mechanistic Shifts: Strong binders (ΔGH* < 0) are limited by product desorption, whereas weak binders (ΔGH* > 0) are bottlenecked by the initial Volmer adsorption step.
  • Non-Precious Alternatives: Identified MoS₂ (ΔGH* = -0.15 eV) as a high-performing non-precious candidate suitable for industrial-scale electrolyzers.
